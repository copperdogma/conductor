## What changed

The original proposal over-weighted cheap production replacements, tiny
admission slices and saturated tasks. It missed maintained architecture-deletion
tests. Treating a five-second or one-cent production miss as a reason to stop
an explicitly authorized capability experiment would answer the wrong question.

Use three separate verdicts: **capability demonstrated**, **practical value**,
and **production/architecture qualification**. Existing production quality,
privacy, cost and latency gates remain authoritative. A quality-only diagnostic
does not waive them or establish deletion of a pipeline. Record their misses
but complete the predeclared quality panel even if cheap-slot economics fail.
Stop for invalid transport that cannot be repaired within scope, hard budget,
ineligible payload or safety boundary; retain individual quality failures and
continue complementary cases rather than treating one miss as universal failure.

Bounded research on the general problem, frontier-model evaluation:

- [Anthropic release](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)
  reports gains on difficult coding, reasoning and vision, with different
  effort/cost frontiers by task. These are provider-reported results, not local
  evidence or proof of a universal smartest model.
- [Anthropic evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
  distinguishes capability from regression evaluation, recommends outcome-based
  grading and balanced tasks, and warns that saturated or restrictive tests
  can hide gains. Adopt these principles; do not invent an unconstrained demo.
- Local applicability check: Storybook C1/C2 are still in package.json and
  spec detection, LC026 metadata explicitly says Synthetic, and Attempt034
  identifies a new subject model as the next lever. Its model helper is not
  Fable-ready: unknown Claude IDs get temperature0, and effort/output ceilings
  are missing. Qualify a bounded owner adapter before inference; an unsupported
  request is not a semantic model failure.
